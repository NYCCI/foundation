**National Youth Coordination and Continuity Initiative**

**Programme Architecture**

Defines the common structure within which individual NYCCI programmes are designed, established, operated, evaluated, and adapted.

NYCCI · Nigeria · 2026

**1.  Programme Purpose**

An NYCCI programme is a structured framework through which young people, institutions, organisations, or communities participate in defined activities toward an identified purpose. Programmes provide the structure through which participation is organised, responsibilities are established, activities are carried out, and resulting work, experience, evidence, relationships, and knowledge can be retained for subsequent use.

A programme may arise from circumstances already present within an environment. A group of Corps Members may identify a community need through their CDS activities and organise a project in response. A university may present a problem affecting its students and invite a youth team to investigate it. A student organisation may require a structured handover when a new leadership team assumes responsibility. A community may retain fragments of knowledge about an earlier youth initiative that are no longer held in a usable form.

NYCCI programmes provide defined structures through which such situations can be addressed. Depending on the programme, this may involve organising participation, undertaking work, investigating a problem, transferring responsibility, recovering previous knowledge, or carrying out other defined activities.

Each programme shall have:

•  a defined purpose;

•  a participating environment;

•  a defined participant model;

•  applicable institutional relationships;

•  defined activities;

•  expected outputs and outcomes;

•  requirements for evidence and documentation;

•  applicable continuity and knowledge-preservation arrangements; and

•  defined means of evaluating the programme.

**2.  Programme Type**

NYCCI programmes shall be classified according to the primary purpose for which they are established. Programme type identifies the principal circumstance or objective that gives rise to the programme. It does not describe every activity undertaken, capability demonstrated, institution involved, or outcome produced during the programme. A programme may include research, training, community activity, documentation, problem-solving, cross-institutional participation, or other forms of work where these activities support its established purpose.

The initial national programme framework comprises four programme types:

•  YC² — Youth Coordination & Community Continuity Project

•  Institutional Challenge Programme

•  Continuity Programme

•  Recovery Programme

**2.1  YC² — Youth Coordination & Community Continuity Project**

YC² is a programme established around an existing recurring youth-service environment through which structured youth participation and community contribution can be organised. YC² operates through the National Youth Service Corps environment and may use existing structures such as Community Development Service (CDS) as points of participation.

For example, Corps Members serving within a locality may identify a community need, form a working team, engage the relevant community or institution, undertake a defined project, and preserve the experience generated through that work for subsequent participants. The programme is designed around the recurring nature of the service environment: participants enter, contribute during their service period, and subsequently leave while the community, institution, or activity may continue.

**Primary purpose:** *Structured youth participation and community contribution within a recurring service environment.*

**2.2  Institutional Challenge Programme**

An Institutional Challenge Programme is established around a defined problem, need, or question presented by an institution, organisation, community, or other participating partner for coordinated investigation or response. For example, a university may identify a recurring problem affecting students and invite a multidisciplinary youth team to investigate its causes, develop possible responses, test an approach, or produce recommendations for the institution.

A challenge may involve research, design, technical development, community engagement, prototyping, or other activities required to address the defined problem. The programme may produce demonstrated capability, institutional knowledge, completed work, relationships, and subsequent opportunities for participants.

**Primary purpose:** *Coordinated investigation or response to a defined problem or challenge.*

**2.3  Continuity Programme**

A Continuity Programme is established where useful understanding, responsibility, or ongoing work must be transferred across a change in people, leadership, participation, or organisational responsibility. For example, when a student organisation's executive committee changes, an outgoing team may work with the incoming team to transfer knowledge concerning previous projects, existing commitments, institutional relationships, unfinished work, operating practices, and lessons from earlier activity.

Continuity activity may also apply where recurring volunteers, project teams, or other participants change while the underlying activity or responsibility continues. The programme may involve documentation, handover sessions, review of previous work, relationship transfer, orientation, or other activities necessary to establish continuity.

**Primary purpose:** *Preservation and transfer of useful understanding across an active transition.*

**2.4  Recovery Programme**

A Recovery Programme is established to recover useful understanding from previous coordination or activity where that understanding has become fragmented, undocumented, inaccessible, or otherwise difficult to use. For example, a community may remember that a youth-led project operated several years earlier but no longer possess a complete record of who participated, what was attempted, what was achieved, what relationships were established, or what was learned.

Recovery activity may include interviews, archival work, collection of surviving records and artefacts, reconstruction of events, verification of accounts, and organisation of the resulting knowledge for subsequent use. A recovered body of knowledge may subsequently support a new project, a continuity process, institutional planning, or another NYCCI programme.

**Primary purpose:** *Recovery and preservation of useful understanding from previous activity whose knowledge has become fragmented or inaccessible.*

**Programme Classification**

Programme classification shall follow the primary purpose for which the programme is established. Activities and outcomes shall not, by themselves, determine programme type. A YC² programme may contain a community challenge, research activity, capability development, or continuity activity. An Institutional Challenge Programme may produce capability evidence and preserved knowledge. A Continuity Programme may require recovery of older records. A Recovery Programme may enable subsequent activity based on the knowledge recovered. These activities and outcomes do not alter the programme's classification where they support its established primary purpose.

Programme characteristics such as state or national scope, university or community setting, cross-institutional participation, multidisciplinary participation, recurring activity, or cohort-based participation shall be specified separately from programme type. Demonstrated capability and subsequent opportunity are outcomes or pathways that may arise from participation across programme types and shall not constitute programme types in themselves.

**3.  Programme Lifecycle**

An NYCCI programme shall progress through a defined lifecycle from identification of the circumstances that warrant its establishment through activity, review, preservation, and continuation or closure.

**3.1  Identification**

The circumstances giving rise to a programme are identified within a participating environment. These may include a defined institutional problem, an impending transition, fragmented knowledge from previous activity, or an existing recurring environment through which structured youth participation can be organised. The circumstances may be identified by a government institution, university, community, youth organisation, programme participant, institutional partner, or NYCCI. The circumstances identified at this stage shall provide the basis for determining whether an existing NYCCI programme type is applicable.

**3.2  Programme Design**

The identified circumstances are translated into a defined programme. The programme design shall establish its primary purpose, programme type, participating environment, participants, institutional relationships, activities, expected outputs and outcomes, evidence requirements, continuity arrangements, and applicable technology requirements. The design shall establish the relationship between the programme's primary purpose and the activities through which that purpose will be pursued.

**3.3  Establishment**

The programme is formally established under the applicable NYCCI programme and governance arrangements. Where participation by an external institution, authority, organisation, or community is required, the relevant relationship shall be established before activities requiring that relationship commence.

**3.4  Mobilisation**

Participants, institutional partners, resources, and other required parties are brought together. Participants shall be oriented to the programme's purpose, their responsibilities, the activities they will undertake, applicable requirements, and the manner in which their participation and contribution will be recorded. Where a programme operates through an existing environment, mobilisation shall account for the structures and participation arrangements already present within that environment.

**3.5  Activity**

The programme carries out its defined activities. Participants undertake the work, service, investigation, transfer, recovery, community activity, or other activity for which the programme was established. Relevant evidence of participation, contribution, decisions, outputs, and outcomes shall be recorded during programme activity.

**3.6  Review**

Programme activity is reviewed to establish what was accomplished, what was learned, what difficulties arose, what evidence was produced, and what may be useful for subsequent activity. Review shall consider the extent to which the programme fulfilled the purpose for which it was established. Where applicable, review may also identify demonstrated capabilities, relationships, unresolved matters, subsequent opportunities, or further activity arising from participation.

**3.7  Preservation**

Relevant understanding and evidence generated through the programme are organised and preserved for future use. This may include records of participants, contributions, outputs, lessons, relationships, decisions, unresolved matters, completed work, and recommendations for subsequent activity. The form and extent of preservation shall correspond to the nature of the programme and the value of the information generated through it.

**3.8  Continuation or Closure**

Following review and preservation, a programme may continue into another cycle, proceed into a subsequent activity, be transferred to another group or institution, be revised and repeated, or be formally concluded. Where a programme continues or is transferred, the relevant preserved understanding shall be made available to those assuming responsibility for the subsequent activity. Closure shall not prevent relevant programme knowledge and evidence from remaining available for future use.

**4.  Programme Design Requirements**

Every NYCCI programme shall be sufficiently defined before formal activities commence.

**4.1  Defined Purpose**

The programme shall identify the specific purpose for which it is established. The purpose shall arise from identifiable circumstances within the intended environment and shall be sufficiently specific to distinguish the programme from other NYCCI programmes. The programme's primary purpose shall determine its programme type in accordance with Section 2.

**4.2  Defined Environment**

The programme shall identify where and within what institutional or social environment it operates. This may include a state, university, community, NYSC service environment, innovation ecosystem, organisation, or combination of environments. Where multiple environments are involved, the programme design shall identify their respective roles.

**4.3  Defined Participants**

The programme shall specify who may participate and the basis on which participation is established. Where participants perform different functions, those responsibilities shall be defined. The participant model shall correspond to the purpose and activities of the programme. For example, a Continuity Programme may require participation by outgoing and incoming leaders, while an Institutional Challenge Programme may require a selected team with the capabilities required to investigate the defined problem.

**4.4  Defined Institutional Relationships**

The programme shall identify the institutions, organisations, communities, or authorities whose participation is required or expected. The nature and purpose of each relationship shall be established before the programme relies upon it. Where an institution presents a problem, provides a service environment, supplies historical information, receives programme outputs, or assumes responsibility for subsequent activity, that role shall be defined within the programme design.

**4.5  Defined Activities**

The programme shall specify the activities through which its purpose will be pursued. Activities shall be sufficiently concrete that participants and participating institutions can understand what is expected of them. For example, a Continuity Programme operating within a student organisation may involve an outgoing leadership team documenting previous projects and commitments, an incoming team reviewing the records, and both groups identifying matters requiring transfer. An Institutional Challenge Programme may involve participants investigating a university problem, consulting affected students and staff, developing a response, and presenting the resulting work to the institution.

**4.6  Defined Outputs and Outcomes**

The programme shall establish what its activities are expected to produce and what those outputs are intended to support. Outputs may include completed projects, research, documentation, community activities, recommendations, technical work, or other tangible results. Outcomes may include demonstrated capability, institutional learning, continued activity, improved coordination, preserved knowledge, relationships established through participation, or subsequent opportunities. Outputs and outcomes shall be distinguished from the activities through which they are produced.

**4.7  Evidence Requirements**

The programme shall identify the evidence required to establish relevant participation, contribution, activity, outputs, and outcomes. Evidence shall be collected in a manner appropriate to the programme and sufficient to support review, preservation, and subsequent use. Where participant contribution may give rise to subsequent opportunity, the evidence shall be sufficiently reliable to support an informed assessment of that contribution.

**4.8  Continuity Requirements**

The programme shall identify the understanding that should remain available after an activity ends or responsibility changes. The design shall account for foreseeable transitions such as graduation, completion of service, change of leadership, withdrawal of participants, transfer of responsibility, or conclusion of a project. Where continuity is not a primary programme purpose, continuity requirements shall nevertheless be defined where the programme generates information or relationships that may be useful for subsequent activity.

**4.9  Technology Requirements**

Where digital infrastructure is required, the programme shall specify the functions required and the point at which those functions enter the programme lifecycle. Digital infrastructure may support participant coordination, activity records, contribution evidence, communication, documentation, preservation, discovery, or other programme requirements. BuzzFeh may provide infrastructure for recording, organising, preserving, connecting, or making programme information available where applicable. The programme shall remain understandable and operational at the programme level regardless of the technical implementation used to support it.

**4.10  State Adaptation**

State programmes may adapt programme design to local institutional structures, needs, resources, and circumstances. Adaptation shall preserve the programme's defined purpose, programme type, and applicable NYCCI requirements. A programme operating through NYCCI Ondo may therefore differ in its activities, participating institutions, participant recruitment, or local implementation from a corresponding programme operating through NYCCI Kwara, while remaining recognisable as the same programme type and operating within the national framework.

**5.  Participant Model**

NYCCI programmes shall define participation according to the contribution, responsibility, or involvement required by the programme. Participants may include young people, project teams, community members, institutional representatives, subject-matter specialists, volunteers, and other persons whose participation is relevant to the programme. The participant model shall correspond to the purpose, activities, and operating environment of the programme.

**5.1  Participant Categories**

A programme may designate participants according to their function:

**Participants:** Persons taking part in programme activities. The broadest category — all other roles below are subsets of it.

**Contributors:** Participants who provide defined work, expertise, or other substantive contributions. Distinguished from general participants by the specificity and accountability of their assigned input.

**Coordinators:** Persons responsible for organising or directing programme activity. Accountable for ensuring that the programme's defined activities are carried out and recorded.

**Institutional Representatives:** Persons participating on behalf of a partner institution. Their role is defined by the institution's relationship to the programme rather than by individual contribution.

**Advisors or Specialists:** Persons providing relevant expertise to a programme without holding operational responsibility for programme activity.

A person may hold more than one function where the programme requires it. For example, YC² may involve Corps Members, CDS structures, community participants, and local institutional representatives. An Institutional Challenge Programme may assemble a selected team around a defined problem. A Continuity Programme may involve outgoing and incoming holders of responsibility. A Recovery Programme may involve former participants, community members, custodians of records, and persons able to provide relevant historical information.

**5.2  Participation**

Each programme shall establish: eligibility; admission or selection requirements; responsibilities; expected contribution; applicable conduct requirements; duration of participation; and conditions for completion or withdrawal. Participation shall correspond to an actual programme activity or responsibility. Where selection is required, the basis for selection shall be appropriate to the programme's purpose and the contribution required.

**5.3  Contribution and Evidence**

Where a participant makes a substantive contribution, the programme shall establish the evidence required to record that contribution. For example, a participant who coordinates a community project may have a record of the team coordinated, activities completed, responsibilities undertaken, and resulting outcomes. A participant who contributes technical work to an Institutional Challenge may have evidence of the specific work completed and its use in addressing the defined problem. A participant involved in a Continuity Programme may contribute by documenting previous work, transferring relationships, or assuming responsibility for ongoing activities. A participant in a Recovery Programme may contribute through interviews, records, photographs, correspondence, or other information used to reconstruct previous activity. The nature and extent of recorded contribution shall depend on the programme.

**5.4  Participant Transitions**

Programmes shall account for changes in participation. Where a participant leaves, completes their involvement, changes responsibility, or is replaced, relevant programme information shall remain available to the persons assuming the associated responsibility. This is particularly important in recurring environments such as student organisations and NYSC service groups, where participants routinely change while activities continue. Where a programme is specifically established to manage such a transition, the requirements of the Continuity Programme shall apply.

**6.  Institutional Partner Model**

NYCCI programmes may operate in partnership with institutions whose participation provides access, resources, expertise, authority, opportunities, or other support required by the programme. An institutional partner may include a government ministry or agency, university, innovation hub, youth organisation, community organisation, private organisation, professional body, or other relevant institution. The role of an institutional partner shall be defined according to the requirements of the programme.

**6.1  Partnership Purpose**

Each institutional relationship shall have a defined purpose. The programme shall identify what the partner contributes to the programme and what the programme requires from the partner. Examples include: providing access to a participating community; presenting a challenge for participants to address; providing facilities or resources; identifying or facilitating participant access; providing technical or subject-matter expertise; supporting programme activities; providing access to records or persons with relevant knowledge; receiving programme outputs; or supporting subsequent opportunities or activity. The purpose of the relationship shall correspond to the programme's established purpose.

**6.2  Institutional Participation**

The appropriate representative of a partner institution shall be identified for programme coordination. Where institutional authority or formal commitment is required, participation shall be established through the applicable institutional instrument or agreement. The extent of institutional involvement may vary between programmes — a partner may provide the environment through which participation occurs, present a defined problem, facilitate a transition, provide access to historical information, receive programme outputs, or perform another defined function.

**6.3  Partner Responsibilities**

A programme shall define the responsibilities of each institutional partner relevant to the activity. Responsibilities may include providing agreed resources, facilitating access, presenting or defining a challenge, identifying relevant participants, providing records or institutional knowledge, reviewing outputs, participating in programme activities, or supporting continuity after an activity concludes. Responsibilities shall be established before the programme relies upon the relevant institutional contribution.

**6.4  Partner Benefits**

The programme shall identify the value created for participating institutions. This may include access to demonstrated youth capability, completed work, research, community engagement, institutional knowledge, recovered records, continuity of ongoing activity, relationships, or opportunities for future collaboration. For example, a university participating in an Institutional Challenge Programme may present a recurring student-services problem to a youth team, receive the resulting work, and retain the knowledge generated during the activity for subsequent institutional use. An organisation participating in a Continuity Programme may receive an organised record of its previous work and a structured transfer of relevant responsibilities to an incoming team. A community participating in a Recovery Programme may contribute historical information and receive an organised record of a previous initiative that can be used in subsequent community activity.

**6.5  Multiple Partners**

A programme may involve multiple institutions where the activity requires participation across institutional boundaries. The programme shall define the respective roles of participating institutions and the relationship between them. For example, a university may provide the challenge, an innovation hub may provide facilities or technical support, and a community organisation may provide access to the people affected by the problem.

**6.6  Partnership Continuity**

Where an institutional relationship produces knowledge, resources, commitments, relationships, or ongoing activities that may be relevant to future programme cycles, those elements shall be documented and preserved in accordance with the applicable NYCCI framework. Where responsibility for an institutional relationship changes, relevant information shall be available to the persons or institutions assuming that responsibility. A subsequent programme cycle should be able to understand the relevant history of the relationship, the commitments already made, the work already completed, and the matters requiring further action.

**7.  Activity Model**

NYCCI programmes shall operate through defined activities undertaken by participants within a specified environment and in pursuit of the programme's purpose. An activity is a defined instance of work carried out within a programme. It is through activities that participants contribute, work is produced, evidence is generated, and programme outcomes arise. A programme may contain one activity or multiple related activities. The activities undertaken shall remain within the purpose and scope of the programme.

**7.1  Activity Definition**

Each activity shall establish: its purpose; the participants involved; the institution or environment concerned; the work to be undertaken; the expected outputs; the period of activity; and the applicable evidence requirements. An activity may consist of a single event or a sequence of related actions. For example, an Institutional Challenge may begin with a briefing by the institution, proceed through team formation, investigation and development of a response, and conclude with the presentation of the resulting work.

**7.2  Activity Formation**

Activities may originate from an identified programme requirement, a participating institution, a participating community, a participant or participant group, an identified need arising during programme activity, or an existing activity requiring continuation or further development. The programme shall determine whether the proposed activity falls within its purpose, scope, and available capacity before it is undertaken. Where an activity introduces a new purpose outside the established programme, the programme shall determine whether the activity requires a change to the programme or should be established separately.

**7.3  Activity Coordination**

Each activity shall have an identified person or group responsible for coordinating its execution. Coordination may include assembling participants, establishing responsibilities, organising meetings or work sessions, managing dependencies, communicating with institutional partners, monitoring progress, and ensuring that required evidence is captured. The extent of coordination required shall depend on the nature and scale of the activity.

**7.4  Activity Execution**

Participants shall carry out the defined work according to the responsibilities established for the activity. Activities may involve project development, research, community service, institutional problem-solving, training, events, documentation, technical work, or other forms of work relevant to the programme. The programme shall allow activities to respond to circumstances that arise during execution while preserving the activity's defined purpose, responsibilities, and required records. Where an activity changes materially during execution, the programme shall determine whether the change remains within the approved activity or requires further definition.

**7.5  Activity Completion**

An activity shall be considered complete when its defined work has been concluded or the programme determines that sufficient work has been completed for the activity to be reviewed. Completion shall include recording relevant outputs, participant contributions, outcomes, and outstanding matters. Where work remains unfinished, the programme shall determine whether it should be continued, transferred, incorporated into a subsequent activity, or formally concluded.

**8.  Evidence & Outcomes**

NYCCI programmes shall distinguish between the following elements:

**Participation:** A person's involvement in programme activity.

**Contribution:** The work or responsibility undertaken by that person.

**Evidence:** A basis for establishing that participation or contribution.

**Outputs:** The identifiable work produced through an activity.

**Outcomes:** The effects or subsequent value arising from the activity.

These elements shall be recorded and assessed according to the purpose and requirements of the programme.

**8.1  Evidence**

Evidence shall be collected where it is relevant to establishing participation, contribution, activity performance, outputs, or programme results. Depending on the programme and activity, evidence may include completed work, project outputs, research, documentation, participant contributions, coordination records, decisions, institutional feedback, photographs or other activity records, demonstrated responsibilities, or records of completed tasks. Evidence requirements shall be proportionate to the nature and scale of the activity.

**8.2  Contribution**

A participant's contribution shall be understood through the work, responsibility, expertise, or other substantive involvement they provide within the programme. For example, a participant who leads a community project may have evidence of the team coordinated, responsibilities managed, decisions made, work completed, and results achieved. A participant who contributes research may have evidence of the research produced, the methods applied, and the manner in which the work informed the activity. A participant who contributes technical work may have evidence of the specific work completed and its use within the resulting project or solution. Participation alone shall not establish a particular capability.

**8.3  Outputs**

Outputs are identifiable products or completed results produced through programme activity. They may include a completed community project, a research report, a developed solution, a documented process, an organised event, training delivered, an institutional resource, or other defined work produced through the activity. Each programme shall identify the outputs relevant to its purpose and activities.

**8.4  Outcomes**

Outcomes describe the effects, changes, or subsequent value arising from programme activity. Depending on the programme, outcomes may include demonstrated capability, new institutional knowledge, continued activity, improved coordination, relationships established between participants and institutions, responses to identified problems, increased institutional visibility into demonstrated youth capability, or subsequent opportunities arising from demonstrated contribution. Outcomes shall be assessed in relation to the purpose for which the programme was established. For example, an Institutional Challenge may result in a university adopting a participant-developed solution. A Continuity Programme may enable an incoming team to continue work without reconstructing its history. A Recovery Programme may provide a community with knowledge that had previously become inaccessible.

**8.5  Preservation of Results**

Relevant evidence, outputs, and outcomes shall be organised and preserved according to their continuing value. The programme shall determine what information should be retained, the purpose for which it may be used, who may access it, how long it should remain available, and what arrangements apply when the programme or activity concludes. Where applicable, BuzzFeh may provide the digital infrastructure through which programme records, contributions, relationships, evidence, and resulting understanding are maintained.

**8.6  Subsequent Use**

Preserved programme results may support subsequent programme cycles, future participants, participating institutions, related activities, programme evaluation, institutional decision-making, and relevant opportunities. A participant's demonstrated contribution may, where appropriate, form part of the evidence considered for a subsequent opportunity. For example, a young person who has contributed technical work to an Institutional Challenge may later be considered by a participating institution for a technical engagement. A participant who has repeatedly coordinated community activities may have evidence of that responsibility available when a relevant leadership opportunity arises. Any use of participant information or evidence for subsequent opportunities shall comply with applicable consent, access, privacy, and programme requirements.

**9.  Continuity & Knowledge Preservation**

NYCCI programmes shall preserve information that may remain useful after an activity, participant, or programme cycle has ended. Continuity concerns the ability of subsequent participants, coordinators, and institutions to understand relevant previous activity and act with the benefit of that understanding. Continuity shall be considered in the design of every programme. A Continuity Programme, however, is established specifically where the preservation and transfer of understanding across a transition constitutes the programme's primary purpose.

**9.1  Continuity**

A programme shall identify information that future participants or institutions are likely to require when assuming related responsibilities. This may include previous activities, decisions and their context, completed and unfinished work, lessons from implementation, participating people and institutions, relevant relationships, resources and working methods, and matters requiring further attention. The information retained shall correspond to the programme's purpose and the responsibilities likely to continue after the activity or programme cycle.

**9.2  Knowledge Preservation**

Programme knowledge shall be recorded in a form that allows authorised future users to understand and apply it. The scope and method of preservation shall follow the circumstances of the programme. A recurring student programme may preserve its previous activities, participating teams, institutional relationships, decisions, unfinished work, and recommendations for the next cycle. A Recovery Programme may preserve reconstructed accounts, historical records, participant testimony, and other material necessary to establish an understanding of previous activity.

**9.3  Transition**

When responsibility passes between participants or groups, relevant programme knowledge shall remain available to those assuming the responsibility. A new coordinator should be able to determine what the previous coordinator did, what remains incomplete, which decisions have already been made, and what has been learned without relying entirely on a personal handover. Where a transition is the primary reason for establishing the programme, the requirements applicable to a Continuity Programme shall apply.

**9.4  Reuse**

Preserved knowledge may inform subsequent activities, programme design, institutional decisions, or other relevant work. The programme should preserve information in a manner that permits subsequent users to understand its context and determine whether it remains applicable.

**9.5  Programme Memory**

Each programme shall establish appropriate arrangements for maintaining its accumulated record. The scope of programme memory shall correspond to the programme's purpose, activities, participants, and applicable privacy and access requirements. Programme memory may include records generated during the programme as well as relevant information received from participating institutions, communities, or previous participants.

**10.  Opportunity Pathways**

NYCCI programmes may create pathways through which demonstrated participation and contribution can lead to subsequent opportunities. An opportunity pathway connects relevant programme evidence with an opportunity for which that evidence may be useful. Opportunity pathways may arise from any NYCCI programme and shall not constitute a separate programme type.

**10.1  Sources of Opportunity**

Opportunities may arise from participating institutions, programme partners, organisations, communities, subsequent NYCCI activities, or other entities connected to programme activity. They may include employment, internships, project participation, leadership responsibilities, fellowships, mentorship, further collaboration, or other relevant opportunities. The form of an opportunity shall depend on the participant's demonstrated contribution and the requirements of the entity offering it.

**10.2  Demonstrated Contribution**

Where a participant's contribution is relevant to an opportunity, evidence generated through programme participation may be used to establish demonstrated experience. For example, a participant who has coordinated a community project, contributed technical work to an Institutional Challenge, or produced research through an NYCCI programme may have evidence relevant to a subsequent opportunity offered by a participating institution. The evidence considered shall relate to the requirements of the opportunity.

**10.3  Opportunity Matching**

Where appropriate, participating institutions or other authorised entities may identify participants whose demonstrated experience corresponds to an available opportunity. Any recommendation or matching process shall be based on relevant evidence and the requirements of the opportunity.

**10.4  Participant Choice**

Participation in an opportunity pathway does not create an obligation to accept an opportunity. Participants shall retain control over whether they pursue an opportunity presented through an NYCCI programme, subject to any applicable programme or institutional requirements.

**10.5  Fairness and Access**

Opportunity pathways shall operate according to defined eligibility and selection requirements. Programme participation alone shall not guarantee employment, selection, advancement, or any other particular outcome. Selection decisions made by external institutions remain subject to those institutions' own requirements and procedures.

**11.  Technology Integration**

Technology shall support the activities, information requirements, and continuity requirements of an NYCCI programme according to the needs of that programme. Technology requirements shall be derived from the activities being undertaken and the information that must be recorded, accessed, preserved, or communicated. The programme shall remain defined by its purpose and activities rather than by a particular technical implementation.

**11.1  Technology Requirements**

Each programme shall identify the functions for which digital infrastructure is required. These may include participant records, activity coordination, assignment and responsibility, communication, evidence collection, project records, institutional participation, knowledge preservation, or access to relevant opportunities. The required technology shall be proportionate to the programme's activities and information requirements.

**11.2  BuzzFeh**

Where a programme requires persistent digital coordination, contribution records, relationship records, evidence, or continuity infrastructure, BuzzFeh may serve as supporting infrastructure. Its use shall correspond to the requirements of the programme. For example, where a programme involves a team working on an institutional challenge, BuzzFeh may support participant coordination, recording of work and contribution, preservation of the activity history, and subsequent access to relevant programme records.

**11.3  Technology and Programme Continuity**

Digital records should support continuity across participant and programme transitions. Where a subsequent group assumes responsibility for an existing activity, the relevant digital record should allow authorised participants to understand the activity's history, current state, outstanding work, and other information necessary to continue the activity.

**11.4  Technology Changes**

Technology may change as programme requirements develop. Where a technical system is replaced or modified, relevant programme knowledge and records shall remain preserved in accordance with applicable NYCCI requirements.

**12.  State Adaptation**

NYCCI programmes shall operate within the national programme framework while accounting for the conditions of the environments in which they are established. State adaptation may reflect differences in institutional structures, youth populations, existing programmes and networks, local needs, available resources, participating organisations, and relevant government or community arrangements.

A State Programme may determine which programme types are appropriate within the state, which institutions should participate, how participants are engaged, and how activities are organised. For example, a programme in Kwara may work closely with universities and innovation hubs because those environments provide established settings for youth-led technical activity. A corresponding programme in another state may operate primarily through community organisations, local institutions, or another established youth environment.

State adaptation shall preserve the purpose and applicable requirements of the underlying NYCCI programme. State-specific arrangements shall be documented where they produce knowledge or experience that may inform programme design or implementation elsewhere. Where a proposed state adaptation would materially alter the purpose or operating requirements of an NYCCI programme, the proposed change shall be reviewed through the applicable NYCCI process.

**13.  Programme Evaluation**

NYCCI programmes shall be evaluated against the purpose for which they were established and the activities, outputs, outcomes, and continuity requirements defined during programme design. Evaluation shall establish whether the programme fulfilled its intended purpose, identify what was accomplished and learned, and determine what changes may be required for subsequent activity or programme cycles.

**13.1  Evaluation Areas**

Depending on the programme, evaluation may consider: participation; completion of activities; quality of outputs; demonstrated contribution; institutional participation; outcomes achieved; knowledge preserved; continuity between programme cycles; subsequent use of programme results; and opportunities arising from programme activity. The relevance of each evaluation area shall depend on the programme's purpose and design.

**13.2  Evidence-Based Evaluation**

Evaluation shall rely on evidence generated through programme activity and other relevant sources. For example, an Institutional Challenge Programme may assess whether the defined problem was adequately investigated, what work was produced, how participants contributed, what the partner institution received, and what should inform subsequent activity. A Continuity Programme may assess whether relevant responsibilities and understanding were successfully transferred. A Recovery Programme may assess the completeness, reliability, and subsequent usefulness of the understanding recovered. A YC² programme may assess the quality and continuity of contribution within the service environment.

**13.3  Participant and Partner Feedback**

Participants and institutional partners may provide feedback on programme design, coordination, activity execution, outputs, and outcomes. Feedback shall be considered alongside programme evidence when determining whether changes are required.

**13.4  Programme Review**

A programme shall be reviewed at appropriate intervals and following significant programme activity or completion. The review may result in continuation, modification, expansion, reduction, transfer, or closure. Material findings from the review shall be preserved where they may inform subsequent programme activity or programme design elsewhere.

**14.  Programme Governance**

Each NYCCI programme shall operate under defined authority and responsibility appropriate to its scope and relationship to the national programme framework. Programme governance shall establish who is responsible for the programme, who may make decisions, how institutional participation is managed, how significant changes are authorised, and how accountability is maintained.

**14.1  Programme Authority**

Each programme shall have an identified authority responsible for its establishment and continued operation within the applicable NYCCI framework. For a State Programme, programme authority shall operate within the governance arrangements established for the relevant state and NYCCI. Programme authority shall not extend beyond the scope assigned to the programme under the applicable NYCCI arrangements.

**14.2  Programme Coordination**

Each programme shall designate a coordinator or coordinating body responsible for day-to-day programme coordination. The coordinator may be responsible for organising activities, coordinating participants, communicating with institutional partners, maintaining programme records, monitoring progress, and preparing programme reviews. Programme coordination shall remain subject to the authority established for the programme.

**14.3  Institutional Roles**

Where institutions participate in a programme, their respective responsibilities, contributions, and decision-making authority shall be established according to the nature of their participation. An institution may provide an environment, present a challenge, provide resources, facilitate access, contribute expertise, receive programme outputs, or perform another defined function. Participation in a programme does not, by itself, confer authority over NYCCI, the programme, or other participating institutions.

**14.4  Programme Decisions**

Programme decisions shall be made by the persons or bodies authorised under the applicable programme arrangements. Significant changes to a programme's purpose, programme type, scope, institutional relationships, participant requirements, or operating structure shall be reviewed and approved through the applicable NYCCI process. Changes affecting the national programme framework shall be referred to the appropriate national authority.

**14.5  Accountability**

Programme coordinators shall maintain sufficient records to support programme evaluation, continuity, accountability, and future programme design. These records should enable authorised persons to understand what the programme was established to accomplish, what activities were undertaken, who participated, what was produced, what outcomes resulted, and what matters remain outstanding.

**14.6  Relationship to the National Framework**

State and individual programmes shall retain their defined local responsibilities while operating within the national NYCCI Programme Architecture. State-level arrangements may determine local implementation, institutional participation, participant mobilisation, and activity structure within the authority provided by the national framework. Where a programme requires an arrangement outside the authority or scope established by the national framework, the matter shall be referred through the applicable NYCCI governance process before the arrangement is adopted.
